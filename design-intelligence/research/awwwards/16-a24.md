## 13. Design Principles Extracted
1. **A studio/brand whose actual value proposition is "distinctiveness release to release" should
   let its digital presence be genuinely distinct release to release**, rather than forcing a uniform
   template that would undercut the actual brand promise. Use when the underlying product portfolio is
   genuinely heterogeneous by design. Avoid for a brand whose value proposition is consistency/
   reliability — there, a rotating identity would undermine trust instead of reinforcing differentiation.
2. **Source promotional imagery directly from the actual creative work being promoted**, rather than
   commissioning separate marketing photography, when the work itself (film stills, key art) is
   inherently strong visual material. Failure mode: doing this with weak source material just surfaces
   the weakness; this pattern depends on the underlying creative asset actually being visually strong.

## 14. Anti-Patterns
Do not adopt a "different identity per release" approach for a brand that actually needs cross-touch-
point consistency to build trust (e.g., a financial product) — this pattern is specifically justified
by A24's brand promise being about creative diversity, not brand consistency.

## 15. Transferable Patterns
- Per-release/per-product bespoke microsite model, unified by shared production process rather than
  shared visual template.
- Sourcing hero imagery directly from the actual creative work being promoted.

## 19. Lessons for AI-Generated UI
Before defaulting to "one consistent template across every product/release," an AI agent should ask
whether the underlying brand's actual value proposition is consistency or diversity — A24's case
argues that when the real product is deliberately heterogeneous, the design system should be a shared
*process* (how sites get built) rather than a shared *template* (what they look like).
