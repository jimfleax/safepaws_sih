## 13. Design Principles Extracted
1. **When promoting an extension of an already-beloved, highly recognizable annual campaign, reuse its
   established visual signature (color, energy) rather than inventing a new one** — brand equity
   compounds. Use for feature extensions/spin-offs of an existing strong campaign. Avoid for a genuinely
   new, unrelated product that would be better served establishing its own identity.
2. **A promotional site for a mobile-only, social/multiplayer feature should sell the social energy of
   the experience, not attempt to replicate the experience itself on the wrong device** — the site's
   job is conversion (open the app, gather friends), not feature parity.
3. **Deliberate typographic overlap can be a legitimate collage-like stylistic device** when the mood
   being conveyed is energetic/celebratory rather than reference/informational — appropriate for
   campaign and event contexts, inappropriate for content where scanning/reading speed matters most.

## 14. Anti-Patterns
Do not reuse a campaign's established visual signature reflexively for unrelated products just because
it's recognizable — this pattern's justification here specifically depends on this being a genuine
extension of that exact campaign, not a borrowed aesthetic applied elsewhere.

## 15. Transferable Patterns
- Established-campaign-color reuse for a feature extension/spin-off microsite.
- "Sell the social energy, not the feature itself" pattern for promoting mobile-only, in-app social
  experiences via a desktop-viewable site.
- Deliberate typographic overlap as an energetic, collage-style device for celebratory/event content.

## 16. Reusable Component Ideas
- Campaign-color-consistent promotional microsite template for feature extensions of an existing brand
  campaign.
- "Get the app to join" conversion-focused layout for promoting mobile-exclusive social features.

## 19. Lessons for AI-Generated UI
When a design task is to promote a feature extension of an already-beloved brand campaign, an AI agent
should default to reusing that campaign's established visual signature rather than inventing a new one,
and should design the promotional surface to sell the *experience* of using the real feature (here:
live, social, competitive) rather than trying to recreate that live experience on a device/context where
it can't actually run.
