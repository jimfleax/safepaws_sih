## 13. Design Principles Extracted
1. **Motion can be a form of editorial discipline, not just a spectacle tool** — restrained, "weighted"
   scroll response and unobtrusive transitions can be the correct choice specifically when the content
   itself (text, editorial argument) is the primary value, and any transition that draws attention to
   itself competes with reading. Use for text-forward, argument-driven content. Avoid as a default for
   content where the *experience itself* (not the reading) is the value proposition — there, more
   expressive motion may be appropriate instead.
2. **Typographic confidence can itself be the primary art-direction statement**, without additional
   ornamentation — a considered type scale, rhythm, and hierarchy can carry a page's entire visual
   identity. Failure mode: mistaking merely large type for "confident" type — confidence in this context
   implies considered scale relationships and rhythm, not simply size.

## 14. Anti-Patterns
Do not read "restrained motion" as "no motion effort" — the described craft is in tuning transitions to
be unobtrusive, which typically requires more design iteration than a default, un-tuned animation
library setting, not less.

## 15. Transferable Patterns
- Reading-subordinate motion (transitions tuned to support, not compete with, editorial content).
- Type-led visual identity with minimal secondary ornamentation.

## 16. Reusable Component Ideas

- Editorial type-scale system as the primary (not secondary) brand-expression device.
