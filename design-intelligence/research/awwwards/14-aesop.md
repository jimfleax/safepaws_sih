## 13. Design Principles Extracted
1. **Restraint itself can be the entire brand differentiation strategy in a visually loud category** —
   in an industry (beauty/personal care) where competitors typically use bright color and aspirational
   lifestyle imagery, a clinical/apothecary aesthetic stands out precisely by refusing those norms. Use
   when a brand's actual product claim is about quality/formulation rather than lifestyle aspiration.
   Avoid in categories where customers specifically expect and respond to aspirational imagery — pure
   restraint there can read as cold rather than premium.
2. **Consistency across every physical and digital touchpoint compounds brand trust** — a customer who
   has experienced the restrained retail environment should find the same visual language online.
   Failure mode: an online presence that doesn't match an established physical brand experience creates
   dissonance and weakens both.

## 14. Anti-Patterns
Do not adopt a muted, restrained apothecary aesthetic as a generic "premium" shortcut disconnected from
an actual product/brand story that justifies it — the aesthetic works for Aesop because it's tied to a
genuine formulation/quality-focused brand narrative, not because muted colors are inherently premium.

## 15. Transferable Patterns
- Clinical/apothecary-influenced restraint as brand differentiation in a visually loud category.
- Cross-channel consistency between physical and digital brand experience.
