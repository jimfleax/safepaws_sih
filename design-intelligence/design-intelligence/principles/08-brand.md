# Brand Expression

## What it is
Making design decisions that are traceably specific to *this* product/company, rather than
interchangeable with any competitor's — the single clearest differentiator between "on-brand" and
"generic but polished."

## The governing test (from research/synthesis/09-brand-expression.md)
Every strongly-evidenced brand decision in this research set can be stated as one sentence connecting
the visual choice to something real about the brand: Cowboy's zero-color chrome → "the product's own
industrial design is the asset"; Resn's "no fixed style" → a stated studio philosophy about craft over
aesthetic signature; MSCHF's deadpan-real-functionality → the actual source of the brand's cultural
relevance. If a proposed brand decision can't pass this test, it's decoration, not brand expression.

## Implementation guidance
- Start from the product's actual differentiator (what's true about this company that isn't true of
  competitors), not from a mood board.
- A brand's "personality" should show up in specific, checkable decisions (word choice, motion timing,
  color discipline), not just an adjective in a brief ("make it feel premium" is not actionable without
  translating to specific decisions).
- Consistency across touchpoints compounds brand trust — see Aesop/Kinfolk's cross-channel consistency
  (general reputation, `research/awwwards/14-aesop.md`, `24-kinfolk.md`).

## Failure mode
"Vibes-based" branding — purple gradients because "SaaS," rounded corners because "friendly," a
particular illustration style because it's currently popular — none traceable to anything specific about
this company. See `anti-generic-ai/generic-ui-signatures.md`.

## When to prioritize category convention over brand distinctiveness
Certain UI moments (checkout flows, form validation, critical safety warnings) benefit more from
matching well-learned user expectations than from brand novelty — see `17-form-design.md` and
tension #2 (clarity vs. novelty) in `research/synthesis/12-design-tensions.md`.
