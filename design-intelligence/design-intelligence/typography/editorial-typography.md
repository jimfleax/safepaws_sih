# Editorial Typography

## What it's for
Content-forward contexts (`principles/21-editorial.md`) where typography itself is the primary craft
statement, not a secondary decoration around other content — By-Kin's confident restraint and Mat
Voyce's type-as-content practice (`research/awwwards/10-by-kin.md`, `11-mat-voyce.md`).

## Distinguishing characteristics from standard UI typography
- Greater willingness to use display/serif or characterful typefaces where a UI context would default to
  a neutral grotesk — appropriate specifically because the reading experience itself is the product.
- More deliberate use of classical editorial devices: drop caps, pull quotes, considered rag (line-break
  quality), hanging punctuation — used sparingly and consistently, not scattered per-article.
- Motion, where present, is subordinate to reading rhythm (`motion/principles.md`, By-Kin's "weighted,"
  unobtrusive scroll) rather than a separate spectacle layer.

## Implementation guidance
Typographic confidence comes from considered scale relationships and rhythm (`line-length-and-rhythm.md`)
applied consistently across an entire publication/site, not from any single flourish — see
`principles/05-typography.md`'s distinction between "confident" and merely "large" type.

## Failure mode
Editorial devices (drop caps, pull quotes) applied inconsistently or without a systemized rule for when
they trigger — reads as arbitrary rather than considered.
