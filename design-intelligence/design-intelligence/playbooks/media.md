# Playbook: Culture / Media / Entertainment

**UX priorities**: distinct identity per release/property where the underlying content genuinely differs
(A24's per-release approach, `research/awwwards/16-a24.md`) — shared *process*, not shared *template*,
when the brand promise is diversity/distinctiveness.
**Visual priorities**: source hero imagery directly from the actual creative work being promoted
(stills, key art) rather than separately commissioned marketing photography, when the work itself is
visually strong.
**Information architecture**: campaign/feature-extension microsites should reuse an already-established
parent brand's visual signature (Spotify Wrapped Party reusing "Wrapped green,"
`research/awwwards/17-spotify-wrapped-party.md`) rather than inventing a new identity each time.
**Typical patterns**: `patterns/storytelling.md` for feature/campaign narratives; bespoke per-release
gallery/hero treatment.
**Appropriate motion**: energetic, campaign-appropriate motion is often correct here (contrast with
editorial's restraint) — match energy to the specific content being promoted, not a fixed house style.
**Accessibility considerations**: captions for all video content; motion-heavy campaign treatments still
need a `prefers-reduced-motion` fallback despite the energetic brand goal.
**Responsive priorities**: campaign microsites promoting a mobile-exclusive feature/experience should
sell that experience rather than attempting to replicate it on desktop.
**Common mistakes**: forcing one visual template across genuinely diverse creative properties; ignoring
an already-established campaign's visual equity when building an extension.
**Quality checklist**: Does the site's identity match the specific property/campaign, not a generic
house style? Is an existing campaign's visual equity reused where this is an extension of it?
