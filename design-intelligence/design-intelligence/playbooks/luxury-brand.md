# Playbook: Luxury Brand

**UX priorities**: restraint as genuine brand argument, not generic "premium" styling — see
`principles/08-brand.md`'s one-sentence test applied to Aesop/Kinfolk/Muji (general reputation,
`research/awwwards/14-aesop.md`, `24-kinfolk.md`, `25-muji.md`).
**Visual priorities**: muted, disciplined palette; generous whitespace; typographic confidence over
decorative flourish; product/material quality carrying visual interest rather than brand chrome.
**Information architecture**: cross-channel consistency matters disproportionately here — a customer's
expectation from physical/other touchpoints should be met exactly, not diverged from digitally.
**Typical patterns**: achromatic or near-achromatic UI (`tokens/themes/luxury.tokens.json`), editorial-
style product presentation over dense merchandising grids.
**Appropriate motion**: slow, deliberate, minimal (`tokens/themes/luxury.tokens.json` motion voice) —
speed/energy motion actively undercuts a considered, unhurried brand feeling.
**Accessibility considerations**: low-contrast "elegant" muted-on-muted text combinations are a common,
real risk in this category specifically — verify actual contrast ratios rather than trusting how
sophisticated the palette looks.
**Responsive priorities**: whitespace/generosity should be preserved proportionally on mobile, not
eliminated first when space gets tight — cutting whitespace before cutting content undermines the exact
brand feeling being built.
**Common mistakes**: muted palette applied as a generic "looks premium" shortcut with no connection to
an actual product/brand story (`principles/08-brand.md`); low-contrast text failing accessibility.
**Quality checklist**: Can the restraint be traced to a specific, real brand story? Contrast verified,
not assumed? Consistency with other brand touchpoints checked?
