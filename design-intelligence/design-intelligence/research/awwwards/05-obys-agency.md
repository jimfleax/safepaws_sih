## 13. Design Principles Extracted
1. **A sparse navigation can be a business-model signal, not just a UX minimalism choice** — if the
   business model is "few, deep client relationships," the site's own simplicity should match, rather
   than performing breadth it doesn't have. Failure mode: a sparse nav on a business that actually needs
   broad discoverability (e.g., a marketplace) just hides content.
2. **Use design as historical/cultural storytelling, not only aesthetic display** — the AIM project
   reframes an entire past art movement through contemporary technique, giving the work a narrative
   reason to exist beyond "look how skilled we are." Use when there's a genuine story to tell. Avoid
   forcing a narrative conceit onto a project that doesn't have one — it reads as pretense.
3. **Consistency of craft across many projects, not virtuosity in one, is what earns "Studio of the
   Year"-tier recognition** — independently confirmed 30+ individual Site of the Day awards across many
   different projects is a stronger signal than any single flashy site. Implication for a design system:
   optimize for a repeatable standard of craft, not a single hero showcase.

## 14. Anti-Patterns
Do not adopt motion/3D as a studio-wide signature without the underlying typographic/grid discipline
independent sources credit as the actual foundation — the motion is described as building on top of a
modernist type/grid system, not replacing one.

than a one-off flourish that can't be sustained across a whole product.
