## 13. Design Principles Extracted
1. **A brand's "slowness" or restraint can be the actual value proposition, not a stylistic default** —
   for a media/lifestyle brand explicitly about unhurried living, generous whitespace and muted color
   directly embody the brand's stated philosophy rather than merely looking tasteful. Use when a brand's
   core promise is genuinely about pace/calm. Avoid applying "slow" visual pacing to a brand whose actual
   value is speed/efficiency — the mismatch would undercut the brand's real promise.
2. **Extend a print-editorial visual identity into digital deliberately, rather than defaulting to
   generic "digital brand" conventions** — preserves the specific visual signature that built the
   brand's recognition in the first place. Failure mode: digital conventions (card grids, badges, bright
   CTAs) applied on top of an editorial identity dilute what made it distinctive.

## 14. Anti-Patterns
Do not adopt muted tones and generous whitespace as generic "premium minimalism" disconnected from an
actual brand philosophy of calm/slowness that justifies it — as with Aesop (#14), the restraint here is
argued to work because it's tied to genuine brand meaning, not because muted palettes are inherently
premium.

## 15. Transferable Patterns
- Print-editorial visual identity extended deliberately into digital rather than replaced with generic
  digital-brand conventions.
- Unhurried, documentary-style photography as brand signature, prioritized over polished commercial
  product photography.
