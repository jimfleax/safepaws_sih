## 13. Design Principles Extracted
1. **Exposing genuine functional/technical detail can itself be the aesthetic**, when a product's
   actual engineering is a source of pride — presenting ports, controls, and internal structure plainly
   rather than concealing them behind styling. Use for technical/creative-tool products where the
   audience values and understands the engineering. Avoid for products where technical exposure would
   read as unfinished or intimidating to a mainstream audience.
2. **A single, disciplined, highly consistent accent color across an entire product line builds
   stronger brand recognition than a varied palette** — independently, repeatedly corroborated as core
   to this brand's recognizability. Failure mode: introducing palette variation "for interest" dilutes
   the exact recognition value that consistency built.
3. **Technical-manual-style typography/iconography (numbered, schematic) can be a legitimate brand
   voice**, not merely utilitarian necessity, when it's applied with the same design rigor as any other
   brand asset. Failure mode: technical-style typography applied inconsistently reads as an oversight,
   not a choice.

## 14. Anti-Patterns
Do not adopt "expose the technical details" as a universal aesthetic — for products where the actual
audience does not value or understand the underlying engineering, exposed technical detail creates
confusion rather than admiration.

## 15. Transferable Patterns
- Functional-detail-as-aesthetic (exposed ports/controls/structure treated as design content).
- Single, highly disciplined accent color maintained across an entire product family.
- Technical/schematic-style typography and iconography as brand voice.

## 19. Lessons for AI-Generated UI
Even with this entry's evidence limitations disclosed, the transferable lesson holds: an AI agent
designing for a genuinely technical product/audience should consider whether exposing real functional
detail (rather than smoothing it into generic "lifestyle" presentation) would build more credibility
with that specific audience, and should test any accent-color usage against the discipline of "would
this still work if used exactly this way everywhere," rather than varying it per section for visual
interest.
