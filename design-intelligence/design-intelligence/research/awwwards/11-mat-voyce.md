## 13. Design Principles Extracted
1. **Split a portfolio's information architecture by the actual nature of the content, not by a single
   universal template** — experimental/personal work and formal client case studies often need
   genuinely different browsing logics (grid-scan vs. linear narrative). Failure mode: forcing both into
   one format either dumbs down the case studies or over-formalizes the experiments.
2. **A specialist's portfolio should be legibly *about* their specialty structurally, not just in
   content** — a kinetic-typography specialist's site treating motion as the actual subject (not a
   decorative layer) is a stronger, more specific statement of expertise than a generically animated
   portfolio template would be. Generalizes to: a 3D artist's portfolio should feel spatial; a sound
   designer's portfolio should foreground audio; etc.
3. **Name "fluid navigation" as a specific goal when a site deliberately mixes structural modes** — the
   goal isn't just "each section works," it's that moving between differently-structured sections
   doesn't feel like leaving the site. Failure mode: distinct section logics implemented as visually
   disconnected sub-sites, breaking the sense of one coherent space.

## 14. Anti-Patterns
Do not adopt "illustrative kinetic typography" as a generic decorative technique disconnected from a
subject where type animation is the actual expertise being demonstrated — applied to, say, a law firm's
site, the same technique would misrepresent what's actually being offered.

## 15. Transferable Patterns
- Multi-mode portfolio (distinct structural logic per content type, unified by consistent navigation).
- "Design the container to be about the specialty" — the site's form should echo the practitioner's
  actual craft.

## 16. Reusable Component Ideas

- Case-study template for commissioned/client work, separated from a personal-experiment gallery.

## 18. Why This Site Is Excellent
It solves the specific brief of a portfolio *for a motion specialist* by making structure and
interaction serve that specialty directly, rather than applying a generic "creative portfolio" template
— reinforced by an explicit, independently confirmed studio case study describing exactly that

## 19. Lessons for AI-Generated UI
When building a portfolio site, an AI agent should ask "what is this person's specific specialty, and
does the site's structure demonstrate it, or merely display it?" — a kinetic typographer's site should
move differently than a photographer's or a copywriter's, and the IA itself (not just decorative
flourishes) should reflect the difference in content type across sections.
