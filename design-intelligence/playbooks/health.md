# Playbook: Health / Wellness

**UX priorities**: trust, clarity, and calm above all — this category should actively avoid anxiety-
inducing visual/interaction patterns (aggressive red alert styling for routine information, alarming
iconography for non-urgent content).
**Visual priorities**: calm, reassuring, and precise — avoid both cold/clinical coldness and
over-familiar/cutesy styling that can undermine trust in sensitive contexts; verify tone against the
actual sensitivity of the content on each specific screen.
**Information architecture**: critical/urgent information must be unmistakably distinguished from
routine/reference information — never let visual hierarchy understate something that needs immediate
attention, or overstate something routine into false alarm.
**Typical patterns**: careful, plain-language content design (`principles/14-content-design.md`) for
anything involving medical information; clear escalation paths to human support for anything the
interface can't resolve.
**Appropriate motion**: minimal and calm — avoid frenetic or urgent-feeling motion outside genuinely
urgent contexts; respect that some users in this category may be actively stressed or unwell while using
the product.
**Accessibility considerations**: especially high priority — users of health products skew toward
higher rates of temporary or permanent disability relative to general population; treat
`principles/12-accessibility.md`'s baseline as a strict minimum, not a target.
**Responsive priorities**: assume interrupted, one-handed, possibly urgent usage contexts on mobile.
**Common mistakes**: alarming visual styling for routine information; playful/cutesy tone applied to
serious medical content; burying an escalation-to-human path behind excessive self-service flow.
**Quality checklist**: Urgency correctly visually calibrated per screen? Plain-language content reviewed
for clarity? Human escalation path always reachable? Tone appropriate to the specific content's
sensitivity, checked per-screen not just once globally?
