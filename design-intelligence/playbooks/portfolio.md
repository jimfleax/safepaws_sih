# Playbook: Portfolio (Personal/Individual)

**UX priorities**: demonstrate the specific specialty as directly as possible — the site's structure
should echo the practitioner's actual craft (Mat Voyce, `research/awwwards/11-mat-voyce.md`), not use a
generic "About/Work/Contact" template regardless of specialty.
**Visual priorities**: a genuine organizing conceit tied to the person's actual identity/practice (Minh
Pham's dual-narrative structure, `research/awwwards/19-minh-pham.md`), not decoration for its own sake.
**Information architecture**: split genuinely different content types (loose experiments vs. formal
client work) into distinct structural modes if they exist, rather than one flat project list.
**Typical patterns**: `patterns/gallery.md`, `patterns/profile.md`, case-study template for commissioned
work.
**Appropriate motion**: can be higher-risk/higher-novelty than most contexts (`principles/22-creative-
web.md`) since the audience is often evaluation/craft-driven — but every device should still map to a
stated concept, and self-check against motion-as-compensation (`research/awwwards/19-minh-pham.md` §8).
**Accessibility considerations**: if pursuing a high-novelty format (spatial/game navigation, per Bruno
Simon), explicitly weigh and disclose the accessibility tradeoff rather than assuming it away.
**Responsive priorities**: high-novelty desktop interactions need an honest, explicit mobile answer —
not an assumption that reflow will work.
**Common mistakes**: generic portfolio template with no connection to the specific specialty; motion/
novelty added to seem impressive rather than to demonstrate actual capability.
**Quality checklist**: Does the site's structure, not just its content, demonstrate this person's
specific specialty? Is any novel interaction's tradeoff a conscious, appropriate choice for this
audience?
