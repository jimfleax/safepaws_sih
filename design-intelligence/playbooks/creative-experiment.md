# Playbook: Creative Experiment / Art Project

Full principle detail: `principles/22-creative-web.md`.
**UX priorities**: this is the one playbook where conventional usability metrics are explicitly not the
primary success criterion — evaluate against the artistic/conceptual goal instead, as Bruno Simon's own
creator did explicitly and publicly (`research/awwwards/07-bruno-simon.md` §11).
**Visual priorities**: full commitment to a single, coherent concept — every decision should trace back
to that one idea (`research/awwwards/07-bruno-simon.md` §18's "every decision must serve one concept").
**Information architecture**: can be entirely non-conventional (spatial/game-world navigation, no
written instruction) specifically because the audience is expected to explore and value the discovery
itself.
**Typical patterns**: `patterns/immersive.md`, `patterns/3d.md`, `patterns/storytelling.md` at their
highest-commitment/highest-risk settings.
**Appropriate motion**: motion IS often the content here, not a supporting layer — the highest
appropriate motion budget in this entire system, still subject to the TRIGGER→MOTION→PURPOSE test
(`motion/principles.md`), just with "artistic/experiential" as a legitimate PURPOSE in this specific
context.
**Accessibility considerations**: explicitly weigh and disclose accessibility tradeoffs rather than
ignoring them — Bruno Simon's own creator and independent press both explicitly named the format's real
limits rather than pretending the concept has no cost; this system asks for the same honesty, not
avoidance of the format.
**Responsive priorities**: an honest, different mobile experience is acceptable here if a genuine
redesign isn't feasible — but the tradeoff should be a stated decision, not an oversight.
**Common mistakes**: treating "creative experiment" as license to skip the concept-commitment discipline
— spectacle with no organizing idea is a failure here too, just measured against artistic coherence
rather than conversion.
**Quality checklist**: Can every major decision be traced to the one core concept? Is the accessibility/
usability tradeoff a conscious, disclosed choice rather than an oversight? Does the audience for this
specific project actually value this level of risk (per tension #2)?
