# Imagery

## What it is
Photography, illustration, video, and 3D content, art-directed as a system — not a stock-photo
placeholder layer.

## Core patterns from this research
- **Source imagery from the actual work being promoted**, not generic marketing photography, when the
  work itself is visually strong — A24's per-release use of real film stills/key art
  (`research/awwwards/16-a24.md` §6).
- **Composition can echo brand identity without literal logo placement** — Stripe's photographic
  logo-echo device (`research/awwwards/01-stripe.md` §6), staging real photography so its geometry
  rhymes with the brand mark.
- **When UI chrome is achromatic, imagery becomes the primary color-carrier** — see `06-color.md`.
- **3D/staged imagery should map to a real concept**, not decorate — Iventions' spotlit-installation
  metaphor tied to an actual events/spaces business (`research/awwwards/21-iventions.md`).

## Implementation guidance
- Every image needs a stated job: does it build trust, demonstrate the product, set mood, or provide
  information? An image with no job is a placeholder, even if it looks polished.
- Crop and aspect ratio should be consistent within a content type (all product shots same ratio, all
  editorial photography same ratio) — inconsistency here is a common, subtle "assembled not designed"
  signal.
- Focal points matter more than resolution — a technically sharp image with a confusing/absent focal
  point performs worse than a lower-resolution image with clear subject emphasis.

## Failure mode
Generic stock photography (visibly stock — posed diversity, exaggerated smiles, obviously staged
"teamwork" shots) or AI-generated illustration used because it's available rather than because it fits
the brand — see `anti-generic-ai/generic-ui-signatures.md` for the specific tells.

## When to prefer illustration/abstraction over photography
Early-stage or abstract products with nothing concrete to photograph yet, or brands whose personality is
better served by a consistent illustrated system than by photography that will vary in quality/
consistency over time — but the illustration style itself still needs the same "what job does this do"
scrutiny as photography.
