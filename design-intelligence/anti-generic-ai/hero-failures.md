# Hero Failures

Full pattern detail in `patterns/hero.md`; this file is the specific failure catalogue.

## The specific signatures
1. **Generic claim headlines** — "Powerful. Simple. Fast." or equivalent copy that could apply to any
   product in any category with no edit (`principles/14-content-design.md`).
2. **Multiple equal-weight CTAs** — two or three buttons of identical visual prominence, giving the eye
   nowhere obvious to land (`principles/01-hierarchy.md`).
3. **Decorative background motion with no purpose** — an animated gradient/particle background with no
   stated communicative role and no performance fallback (`motion/motion-anti-patterns.md`).
4. **A hero that doesn't say what the product actually does** — relying entirely on mood/vibe with no
   concrete claim, leaving a first-time visitor unclear on the basic value proposition.

## The repair
1. Rewrite the headline to make a claim specific to this product that a competitor's headline couldn't
   also honestly make.
2. Establish one primary CTA per hero; demote any secondary action visually (outline/ghost button, or
   move it below the fold).
3. If background motion exists, state its purpose in one sentence (per `motion/principles.md`) or remove
   it; add a static fallback regardless.
4. Verify a first-time visitor unfamiliar with the product could state, after reading only the hero,
   what it does and for whom.

## When mood-forward, low-information heroes are correct
Brand-awareness or creative-portfolio contexts (`principles/22-creative-web.md`) where the audience is
evaluating craft/feeling rather than needing an immediate functional claim — Bruno Simon's portfolio
(`research/awwwards/07-bruno-simon.md`) has no explanatory headline at all, and that's appropriate for
its specific, self-aware, narrow audience.
